many of us had this romantic intuition that optimal algorithms tend to be simple.

maybe because of the sorting and binary search lower bounds. maybe because dijkstra, 3sum, APSP, Christophedes, etc. stood strong for decades and decades.

classic problems, simple algorithms. it feels destined

but now it feels more like efficiency gains are hiding all over the dark corners of the algorithmic search space. the further out you go - the more complex the program grows - the more likely you bump into one

the intuition that optimality favors simplicity was... convenient for humans. we like to believe that Erdos' "The Book" exists for algos too. simple is what we can come up with, prove, and grasp. we don't want to feel left out.

that bias could be behind the p!=np belief

i think agents have ventured into the dark way further than humans; you look at the Jacobian conjecture, and it looks like the counterexample was not that far out. Humanity just didn't venture in that direction.

Somehow agents don't share the disdain for complexity, despite being trained on us. RL turned them into bloodhounds. I saw it on my own research. They tried things that made me cringe to think of trying myself. and some worked.

so that's why i won't be surprised at all if p=np. the emerging evidence is that the algorithmic search space is not as inert as we thought. maybe even the current agents have still a tiny observable universe

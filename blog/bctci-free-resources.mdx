[Beyond Cracking the Coding Interview](https://www.amazon.com/dp/195570600X) comes with quite a lot of accompanying free resources.
Here's a comprehensive list of everything available online (I created it because I was starting to lose track myself!).

<Toc />

## Book content

- **Free chapters from the book**: [bctci.co/free-chapters](https://bctci.co/free-chapters). Get a sense of the book's content with **9 free chapters**: The first 7 chapters, covering topics such as why technical interviews are broken, what recruiters won't tell you, and how to get in the door at companies without a referral, and 2 technical chapters: Binary search and Sliding Windows.
- **Online-only chapters**: These are [Tier 3 topics](https://bctci.co/topics-image) that are available for free online.
  - [bctci.co/union-find](/blog/union-find.mdx)
  - [bctci.co/monotonic-stacks-and-queues](/blog/monotonic-stacks-and-queues.mdx)
  - [bctci.co/set-and-map-implementations](/blog/set-and-map-implementations.mdx)
  - More to come in the future!
- **Online platform**: [bctci.co](https://bctci.co). The main site for the book, with:
  - All the book problems with solutions in **Python, C++, Java, and JavaScript**. (**Pro tip**: You can go from a book problem to the online content by creating a URL with the problem name. E.g., "String Matching" -> [bctci.co/string-matching](https://bctci.co/string-matching))
  - Our AI Interviewer, where you can practice all the book's problems: [bctci.co/ai](https://bctci.co/ai)
  - Dozens of mock interview replays (be a fly on the wall and learn from others!)
  - And more!

<Callout>
  For the main platform, you'll have to create an account if you don't already
  have one, but there's nothing else you need to do to access all the things.
</Callout>

## Practice tools

In the "How To Practice" chapter, we talk about how to make the most of your practice. Our advice includes using the following tools:

- [Postmortem Log Template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1phKTGfnQtuElTQ4BQSBfa1H5QO1-Ip9j8NRVZkR_FTk/edit?usp=sharing) (Google Sheets): After doing a practice interview, we recommend filling in a postmortem log to reflect on what you could have done differently and identify things to work on. [Here](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1LG-aUqjEbTVddkjeuCHMtROlm9BMIhDb/view?usp=drive_link) is an example:

<BlogImage
  src="/blog/bctci-free-resources/postmortem-log.png"
  alt="A postmortem log example"
  width="90%"
/>

- [Python-Based Interview Cheat Sheet Example](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1LtXh1oew6pZ9D4s5mw_33jzA2UwBfnv9jWh1bkSRTCc/edit?usp=sharing) (Google Docs): We recommend maintaining a personalized cheat sheet where you write down things like:

  - Useful recipes and tricks
  - Things you tend to forget
  - Your favorite implementation of classic algorithms
  - Language-specific quirks
  - etc.

The key is to make it your own (you learn by curating it), but you can use our example for reference (it's Python-focused, but yours doesn't have to be). Maintaining this cheat sheet goes a long way in preventing getting rusty!

- [Bug List Example](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1mtzLfvsp1h1LQ8XEMnagzltPVP4OJ2uJ78jHFU-gH2Q/edit?usp=sharing) (Google Docs): We also recommend maintaining a list of bugs you make often. Tracking them like this helps you break out of bad habits.

## Reference documents

- [Reusable ideas](../public/blog/bctci-free-resources/reusable-ideas.pdf): A list of reusable DS&A techniques from the book. It is **not an exhaustive list** of every technique we teach in the book. In particular, it doesn't include any of our code recipes (templates).

- [Trigger Catalog](../public/blog/bctci-free-resources/trigger-catalog.pdf): A list of triggers for each topic from the DS&A Catalog. It is **not an exhaustive list**, as it would be impossible. What we recommend is that when _you_ find a trigger during practice that _you_ find useful, add it to your personalized cheat sheet.

When I wanted to do a PhD in the US, I searched for open problems on professors' personal websites and tried to solve them. I found one - on [David Eppstein](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~eppstein/)'s website - that seemed doable, and managed to solve it with help from [Guillem Godoy](https://www.cs.upc.edu/~ggodoy/), the professor I was working for at the time.

I sent him an email with the solution.[^emeritus] I didn't hear back until, four months later, someone asked the same question on [CS Theory Stack Exchange](https://cstheory.stackexchange.com/questions/25048/hidden-path-in-square-grids), which jogged his memory about my email:

## The problem

The problem was on a slide from one of Eppstein's old courses, listed among project ideas with the note _"Should be NP-complete. No proof known."_

You're given an `n×n` matrix of 0's and 1's and a binary sequence, and asked whether there's a path through adjacent cells, visiting each exactly once, whose values spell out the sequence.

To prove it NP-hard, I reduced from [Hamilton Path on grid graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_path). Here is the original write-up that I emailed him:
